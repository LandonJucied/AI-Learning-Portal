# AI Ethics and Responsible Use
Source: AI Learning Portal educational content
Topics: privacy, bias, academic honesty, copyright, misinformation, deepfakes, accountability.

Responsible AI use requires checking accuracy, respecting privacy, following applicable rules, considering fairness, and taking responsibility for decisions. A human remains accountable for how AI output is used.

Bias can appear when training data, labels, system design, or deployment conditions disadvantage particular people or groups. A system can perform differently across contexts. Ask who is represented in the data, who may be harmed, and how results are evaluated.

Privacy means limiting the collection and disclosure of personal information. Do not enter sensitive or identifying information into a tool without a clear, approved reason. Prefer anonymized examples and follow school, family, and organization policies.

Academic honesty means following the teacher’s assignment rules. If AI is allowed for brainstorming but not for writing the final submission, use it only for brainstorming and create the final work yourself. When disclosure is required, explain what tool was used and how.

Copyright and licensing matter. The fact that material is online does not automatically grant permission to reuse it. Prefer public-domain or appropriately licensed resources, attribute sources, and follow the terms of use.

Deepfakes are synthetic or manipulated media that can make people appear to say or do things they did not. Misinformation is false or misleading information regardless of intent; disinformation is deliberately spread to deceive. Verify surprising claims and media with reliable, independent sources before sharing.

A responsible decision process: identify the purpose; check permission and rules; protect private information; verify factual claims; consider bias and potential harm; disclose AI involvement when required; retain human judgment.
